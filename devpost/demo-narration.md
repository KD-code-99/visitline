# Visitline demo transcript

AI-assisted narration, spoken by Microsoft Zira Desktop. The venue and measurements are fictional.

## Know what to expect before you go

Visitline helps an event organiser turn access details into a clear plan before someone leaves home. This is a working, fictional example, using actual app captures. For this visit, the person needs no steps, ninety centimetres of space, and a way to a toilet.

## A known obstacle has a clear reason

Start with the front entrance. It has three steps, so it does not meet this person's needs. The plan shows the reason and keeps the other parts of the visit visible.

## Missing information stays visible

The courtyard has no steps, but its doorway has not been measured. That is a different problem: it needs checking. Visitline does not treat a blank measurement as a match. It points the organiser to the next detail to check.

## Add a measurement and its source

The organiser enters ninety-six centimetres and explains how the space was measured. These numbers and notes are made up for the demonstration. Saving this detail updates the route result and the visitor card from the same record.

## The recorded details now match

The courtyard now matches the selected ninety-centimetre need at every included part. The wording refers to the organiser's notes and these particular needs. It is not a guarantee that the venue suits every visitor.

## Change one need. Recheck every part.

Now change the space needed to one hundred centimetres. Both the ninety-six-centimetre doorway and the ninety-five-centimetre way to the toilet change to does not match. A suitable entrance alone is not enough to explain the whole visit.

## Share the facts behind the result

The visitor card includes the chosen needs, route details, dates, notes and limits. Download the card for reading and printing, and keep the editable plan for later changes. Files are snapshots: they do not update themselves. The app needs no account or model API, and working details stay in the tab.

## A small, working proof of concept

The Devpost Learn Skill Pack guided substantive planning documents, an app map and a bounded, AI-assisted build. Ten automated tests and actual browser checks cover the core behaviour. Feedback about confusing wording led to plain-language controls. Next comes testing with real organisers and disabled visitors. Visitline compares observations; it does not certify accessibility or safety.
